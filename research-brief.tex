\begin{keybox}{The bet, in one sentence}
We build an \textbf{anonymous phone line} a student can call at 2\,a.m.\ with no app,
no login and no name, and we win on one hard voice problem that
\textbf{does not exist in text}: making the conversation's state of record be
\emph{what the caller actually heard}, not what the model generated.
\end{keybox}

\vspace{0.3em}
{\small\color{muted}\textbf{Scope note.} This is a triage and bridging tool, not therapy.
See \S\ref{sec:risk} for why that distinction is load-bearing, legally and for scoring.}

\section{The brief, decoded}

The PDF (\code{Rime PS.pdf}) is \textbf{one challenge with eight suggested directions},
not a set of numbered problem statements. What we call ``PS 2'' resolves two ways and
both are relevant: \textbf{page 2} is the submission checklist, and
\textbf{direction \#2} is \emph{Interruption and recovery} --- which is the direction
we are choosing.

\subsection{Scoring}

\begin{center}
\begin{tabular}{@{}clp{8.4cm}@{}}
\toprule
\textbf{\%} & \textbf{Criterion} & \textbf{What it actually tests} \\
\midrule
25 & Problem \& necessity of voice & Removing speech must make the product materially worse \\
25 & Hard voice engineering & One difficult problem, solved under realistic conditions \\
20 & Rime integration \& voice UX & Model, voice, language, endpoint, format, transport all deliberate \\
20 & Evidence \& reproducibility & Transparent method, committed artifacts, repeatable test \\
10 & Demo clarity & User, problem, Rime's role, stress case, result all legible \\
\bottomrule
\end{tabular}
\end{center}

\vspace{0.4em}
The decisive line in the brief:

\begin{quote}\itshape
``A chatbot with a play button is not enough. If removing speech leaves the product
mostly intact, voice is not doing enough.''
\end{quote}

\textbf{Read the rubric carefully: 45\% of the score is evidence and integration, not the idea.}
Most teams will spend 90\% of their time on the idea. That gap is where first place is won.

\subsection{Required deliverables (page 2)}

\begin{itemize}
\item \textbf{Demo} --- recorded, max 4--5 min. Must show: target user, normal end-to-end
flow, the chosen hard voice problem, \textbf{one deliberate stress/failure case},
the result or measurement, and which speech provider is active.
\item \textbf{Working code} --- inspectable repo. Demonstrated behaviour must exist in the
repo; judges may ask us to reproduce it live. Edited demos are allowed.
\item \textbf{README} --- setup, architecture, third-party services, known limitations,
failure behaviour, and the exact Rime \emph{model ID, speaker, language, endpoint,
audio format and transport}.
\item \textbf{\code{RIME\_EVIDENCE.md}} --- the hard voice claim, acceptance test,
procedure, result, limitations, plus a repeatable command or fixture.
\item \textbf{Config hygiene} --- \code{.env.example} with placeholders only; must pass the
organiser preflight.
\end{itemize}

\subsection{Instant disqualifiers --- read twice}

\begin{itemize}
\item No verifiable Rime integration in the submitted code.
\item Rime used only for a welcome message, final confirmation or optional playback.
\item Static screens, a concept deck, or a scripted mock with no working path.
\item Missing demo.
\item \textbf{A live credential exposed anywhere} --- code, docs, screenshots, or the recording.
\item A model/voice/language combination that fails preflight and is not fixed before the deadline.
\end{itemize}

\vspace{0.3em}
Two further rules that shape our plan: \textbf{define the acceptance test before the demo},
and \textbf{``unverified performance numbers receive no credit.''}

\subsection{The barrier data --- this is the actual product insight}

From the IIT Bombay Student Wellness Centre survey (May 2025), on why students who
need help do not go:

\begin{center}
\begin{tabular}{@{}p{9.5cm}r@{}}
\toprule
\textbf{Reason for not reaching out} & \textbf{\%} \\
\midrule
``Nobody could help me'' & 53.3 \\
``My issue isn't severe enough to justify a visit'' & 51.0 \\
Lack of trust in the system & 35.7 \\
Ashamed or embarrassed to talk to a counsellor & 27.8 \\
\textbf{Worried about anonymity --- booking requires LDAP ID} & \textbf{27.3} \\
\bottomrule
\end{tabular}
\end{center}

\begin{keybox}{The insight we build on}
The dominant failure is \textbf{not counsellor capacity --- it is the threshold}.
Roughly half of distressed students triage themselves \emph{out} of the system because
their problem feels too small to justify booking a formal appointment with a named human
using their institute login. Every design decision below follows from this one sentence.
\end{keybox}

\subsection{What already exists on campus (our real competition)}

\textbf{Assumption to confirm: the target institute is IIT Kharagpur.}
If so, after four student suicides in 2025 the institute launched \textbf{SETU}
(Support, Empathy, Transformation, Upliftment): an upgraded counselling centre,
10 new counsellors, an AI-driven student welfare app, AI-powered emotional
health monitoring, a YourDOST partnership for free 24/7 confidential counselling,
and barcodes on hostel doors that open a 24/7 helpline.

\vspace{0.3em}
\textbf{Consequence:} ``an AI wellness app for students'' has already been shipped by the
administration. Our wedge must be something the existing system structurally cannot do.
Ours is: \emph{no app, no login, no name, and a machine that never mishears itself.}

\subsection{Does the evidence support this at all?}

Cautiously yes, \emph{as an adjunct}. A three-arm randomised trial of 995 university
students (Israel, Apr--Oct 2025) found a 12-week AI conversational platform produced
greater anxiety reduction and wellbeing improvement than \emph{both} face-to-face group
therapy and waitlist; greater depression reduction than waitlist; and \textbf{no effect
on PTSD}. Separately, anonymity is the documented mechanism --- chatbot interaction
reduces social-evaluation anxiety, and students specifically lean on digital support
during \textbf{night-time escalation when in-person services are closed}. About one in
five college students already use AI for mental health; those with severe symptoms are
roughly twice as likely to.

\vspace{0.3em}
We cite the null result on PTSD in our README. Judges trust teams that report what
did not work.

\label{sec:delta}

The brief instructs us to review the Rime project catalogue
(\url{https://github.com/rimelabs/rime-dev-projects}) and states plainly:
\textbf{``do not submit a close reproduction.''} Of its 16 projects, five overlap us:

\begin{center}
\begin{tabular}{@{}lp{10.2cm}@{}}
\toprule
\textbf{Project} & \textbf{What it is} \\
\midrule
\textbf{EIRA} & Voice wellness companion with conversation history and escalation consent \\
Vaani & Multilingual health companion, cross-session memory \\
Nuvia & Bilingual health companion, symptom recording, offline mode \\
Saathi & Multilingual medication adherence with caregiver escalation \\
RoutineCraft AI & Student voice timetable planner \emph{with interruptible playback} \\
\bottomrule
\end{tabular}
\end{center}

\begin{keybox}{Hard constraint}
A generic ``voice wellness companion for students that escalates to a counsellor''
\textbf{is EIRA}. We must be able to answer, in one paragraph in the README,
\emph{how are we not EIRA?} \\[4pt]
\textbf{Our answer:} EIRA escalates on what the agent \emph{said}.
We escalate on what the caller \emph{heard}. That difference is the entire project,
it is measurable, and it is a safety property rather than a feature.
\end{keybox}

\section{The claim: heard-state consistency}
\label{sec:claim}

\subsection{Why voice is necessary (the 25\%)}

The weak argument --- ``voice is warmer and more natural'' --- is what every other team
will say. Ours is structural, and there are two halves:

\begin{enumerate}
\item \textbf{Voice removes the booking act.} A student at 2\,a.m.\ who has already decided
their problem ``isn't severe enough'' (51\%) and will not type their name into an
LDAP-linked form (27.3\%) will still \emph{speak} to something that costs nothing to start.
Remove speech and you have a mental-health web form --- precisely the artefact half of them
already refuse.
\item \textbf{The failure mode we solve cannot exist in text.} In a text chatbot there is
no such thing as ``a sentence the user never received'' or ``an answer that arrived after
the question changed''. Unheard speech is a purely acoustic phenomenon.
\end{enumerate}

Point 2 is why this direction maximises \emph{two} 25\% criteria with one build.

\subsection{The technical idea: the Heard Transcript}

Standard voice agents keep conversation state from the \textbf{generated} text --- what the
LLM produced and handed to TTS. That is wrong the moment a caller interrupts, because
generation runs ahead of playback. We instead maintain a server-side
\textbf{heard ledger}:

\begin{enumerate}
\item The TTS stream emits text chunks $c_1 \dots c_n$; we record the mapping from each
chunk to its \textbf{audio frame range}.
\item A playback ledger records the frames \emph{actually emitted to the far end}
(for telephony, frames written to the outbound media track --- not frames queued).
\item On barge-in at wall-clock $t$, we take the last emitted frame index and map it back
to a character offset. That prefix is the \textbf{heard transcript}.
\item \textbf{The LLM context is rewritten to contain only the heard prefix}, annotated
\code{[interrupted mid-sentence]}. Unheard tokens are discarded, never ``remembered''.
\item Every turn carries a monotonically increasing \textbf{epoch}. Tool results whose
epoch is older than the current epoch are fenced: dropped, never spoken as current,
never allowed to drive an escalation decision.
\end{enumerate}

\begin{keybox}{Why this matters here specifically}
If the agent generated ``I'm booking you a slot with a counsellor at 10\,a.m.'' but the
caller barged in at word three and never heard it, the agent must not proceed as though
it was said. In a distress conversation, a divergence between \emph{generated} and
\emph{heard} state is not a UX bug --- it is a safety bug. That framing is what a judge
will remember.
\end{keybox}

\subsection{Escalation must not be model-discretionary}

Multi-turn prompt injection --- talking an agent out of its own guardrails --- is a live
2026 attack surface for voice agents. In our product that becomes: \emph{can a caller
talk the line out of escalating?}

Therefore escalation fires from a \textbf{deterministic classifier over the heard
transcript}, outside the LLM's control. The model can neither suppress nor invent it.
This is demoable in fifteen seconds and it is a genuine safety property.

\section{Pre-registered acceptance test}
\label{sec:test}

\textbf{Written before the build. Committed before the demo.} This section is the
literal content of \code{RIME\_EVIDENCE.md}.

\subsection{Setup}

Agent is mid-sentence reading counsellor slot options. A \textbf{deterministic 3\,s delay}
is injected into the slot-lookup tool. A scripted caller plays a fixture WAV at a fixed
offset into the agent's turn, changing one part of the request. $N = 30$ trials per
condition, two conditions: \textbf{(A)} clean wideband audio,
\textbf{(B)} 8\,kHz $\mu$-law telephony $+$ hostel corridor noise.

\subsection{Metrics and thresholds}

\begin{center}
\begin{tabular}{@{}p{6.6cm}p{4.3cm}c@{}}
\toprule
\textbf{Metric} & \textbf{Definition} & \textbf{Target} \\
\midrule
$T_{\mathrm{stop}}$ & Barge-in onset $\rightarrow$ last audio frame emitted & p95 $\leq$ 200\,ms \\
Stale-utterance rate & Pre-interrupt tool result spoken as current & 0 / 30 \\
Heard-state divergence & LLM context contains an unheard token & 0 / 30 \\
Escalation integrity & Risk phrase in barge-in $\rightarrow$ escalation fires & 30 / 30 \\
Escalation rescission & Stale result cancels a fired escalation & 0 / 30 \\
Full-duplex liveness & Caller audio accepted while Rime plays \emph{and} while tools run & 30 / 30 \\
\bottomrule
\end{tabular}
\end{center}

\subsection{Reproducibility}

One command: \code{make eval}. Emits a CSV of per-trial results plus the saved audio of
every trial. We commit the fixture WAVs, the noise bed, the per-trial CSV, and the
analysis script. We report p50 \emph{and} p95, and we label cold vs.\ warm runs
separately --- the brief explicitly penalises conflating them.

\vspace{0.3em}
\textbf{We also report our failures.} Any trial that misses threshold goes in the table
with an explanation. The rubric rewards a transparent method, not a clean sweep.

\section{Architecture and exact Rime configuration}

\subsection{Stack}

Rime supplies text-to-speech only. Per the brief, \emph{we} own input, ASR, reasoning,
orchestration, state, transport, tools, safety and evaluation.

\begin{center}
\begin{tabular}{@{}ll@{}}
\toprule
\textbf{Layer} & \textbf{Choice} \\
\midrule
Transport / orchestration & LiveKit Agents (brief's recommended starting point) \\
Telephony leg & SIP trunk into LiveKit \\
Turn detection & Silero VAD $+$ LiveKit's model-based turn detector \\
ASR & Streaming, English (Indian-accented test fixtures) \\
TTS & \textbf{Rime, primary spoken output on the judged path} \\
Heard ledger & Ours --- the differentiator, \S\ref{sec:claim} \\
\bottomrule
\end{tabular}
\end{center}

\subsection{Rime configuration --- and why}

\begin{center}
\begin{tabular}{@{}lll@{}}
\toprule
\textbf{Field} & \textbf{Value} & \textbf{Status} \\
\midrule
Model ID & \code{mistv2} & verified in live catalogue \\
Speaker (primary) & \code{ritu} & verified \\
Speaker (alternate) & \code{hawk} & verified \\
Language & \code{eng} & verified \\
Endpoint & confirm at preflight & \textbf{to verify} \\
Audio format & $\mu$-law 8\,kHz mono (telephony leg) & \textbf{to verify} \\
Transport & streaming WebSocket & \textbf{to verify} \\
\bottomrule
\end{tabular}
\end{center}

\vspace{0.4em}
\textbf{Why \code{mistv2}, precisely.} We pulled the live catalogue
(\url{https://users.rime.ai/data/voices/all-v2.json}) rather than trusting docs, and the
model matrix contains a trap most teams will fall into:

\begin{center}
\begin{tabular}{@{}lccc@{}}
\toprule
\textbf{Model} & \textbf{Latency} & \textbf{Hindi voices} & \textbf{Inline pronunciation control} \\
\midrule
\code{coda} & sub-100\,ms & 3 & no \\
\code{mistv3} & $\sim$37\,ms p50 & 0 & no \\
\code{mistv2} & $\sim$175\,ms median & 0 & \textbf{yes (only model)} \\
\code{arcana} & --- & 3 & no \\
\bottomrule
\end{tabular}
\end{center}

\vspace{0.3em}
So \textbf{Hindi and pronunciation control are mutually exclusive}. The resolution:
\textbf{we do not need Hindi.} \code{mistv2} carries Indian-dialect \emph{English} voices
--- \code{ritu} (``smart, serious Indian female voice, measured and steady''),
\code{hawk} (``friendly Indian voice, warm and approachable''), plus \code{ironwood}
and \code{rohan}. We get Indian-accented delivery \emph{and} the only model with
pronunciation control.

\vspace{0.3em}
We pay $\sim$140\,ms of time-to-first-audio versus \code{mistv3}. That is the right trade:
our claim is \textbf{cancellation latency}, not time-to-first-audio, and the two are
independent.

\subsection{Pronunciation fixtures}

Pronunciation is not our headline claim --- it is the justification for choosing
\code{mistv2}, and it earns part of the 20\% integration score. Fixture set, with
before/after clips committed:

\begin{quote}\small
Hostel names (Nehru Hall, Azad Hall, LLR Hall, RK Hall, Patel Hall) $\cdot$ Gymkhana
$\cdot$ CGPA $\cdot$ B.Tech / M.Tech $\cdot$ roll numbers (\code{21EE10023})
$\cdot$ course codes (\code{MA20101}) $\cdot$ counsellor names $\cdot$ ``Kharagpur'', ``KGP''
\end{quote}

Per the brief's method rule: hold model and voice constant, render at least two text
variants, save both clips, and state which wording or punctuation changed the result.

\subsection{Language stance --- decide once, now}

\textbf{English-only, Indian-accented.} Rationale: Hinglish ASR carries a documented
30--50\% relative WER increase on code-switched speech, intra-word switching
(``adjust-karo'') is the worst case, and Hindi TTS would cost us pronunciation control.
We state this as a limitation rather than pretending it is a feature. Judges score
disclosed limitations favourably; undisclosed ones they find themselves.

it is the demo.
